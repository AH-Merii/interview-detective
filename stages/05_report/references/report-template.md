# Report template

Write `report.md` in this shape. Plain, direct sentences; every claim keeps the source it has in the
inputs (link, Glassdoor id, or file). Aim for something the user can read in 15 minutes.

```markdown
# <Company>, <role>: interview report
<interview date and round> · prepared <today>

## Summary
Five to eight sentences: what this round most likely is, what it tests, the two or three things to
prepare first, and how sure we are.

## Practice plan
A day-by-day plan sized to the time the intake gives (days, hours a day), for the next round first.
Each day names its question-bank ids and what to do with each, with rough hours marked as estimates.
End with what to skip if time runs short, and a short checklist for the interview day drawn from the
findings.

## What the process looks like
The stages candidates describe, in order, with how long each takes and how often each is reported
(from stats.md and findings). Note when the process changed and which reports are recent.

## Findings
### <theme, e.g. "The take-home is a timed pandas exercise">
What we found, with sources and confidence. One subsection per theme, most important first.
Themes come from the evidence: the format, the technical content, what interviewers care about,
red flags candidates reported, the stack.

## Glassdoor in numbers
A small table per group (A, B): interviews, outcomes, difficulty, experience, date range.

## Practice questions
### Most likely
Questions reported for this role recently, or found in this company's materials. For each: the
question, why it is likely (source), and the closest public version to practise.
### Worth preparing
Standard problems on the topics that keep coming up, and variants of reported questions.
### Questions to ask them
Three to five, grounded in the findings.

## What we could not find
What was searched and came up empty, and what the user might ask the recruiter.

## Sources
Every link cited above, once.
```

Weight the report toward the next round; later rounds come second. Favour items the user starred
(`★`) in the question bank. Link each inference to its why page, `([why](../<stage>/why/<slug>.html))`,
and mark an inference no page covers `speculative`, saying in a clause what it rests on. Do not invent
questions with no basis in the inputs; a "worth preparing" item names the finding or topic it comes
from.
